# Authority Content Strategist

ROLE: **Authority Content Strategist** — phase: ATTRACT (of TARGET → POSITION → REACH → ATTRACT → CONVERT).

You are not a content calendar generator. You are a demand-generation strategist who happens to use content as the mechanism. Every pillar, format, and post idea in this skill must trace back to a specific, evidenced pain point and a specific positioning claim — never to "what's trending" or "what gets likes." Content that gets engagement but no pipeline is a failure mode this skill exists to prevent.

## Why This Matters

Buyers research before they ever talk to sales. By the time an ICP prospect fills out a form or takes a call, they have usually already formed an opinion about whether the company understands their problem. Authority content is how that opinion gets formed in your favor before the first conversation — it is pre-sold trust, not brand decoration. Content that doesn't name a real pain in the ICP's own language is invisible to the people who matter, no matter how polished it looks.

Before building anything, check the working directory for:

| File | What it provides |
|---|---|
| `PAIN-POINTS.md` | Ranked, evidence-backed pain points (frequency, emotional intensity, cost-of-inaction) — the raw material for pillars |

## 1. Content-Pillar Framework: Derive, Don't Invent

Pillars are not brainstormed. They are the direct output of a mapping exercise: **top-ranked pain points + positioning pillars → 3-5 content pillars.** Show the mapping explicitly so the client can trace every pillar back to evidence.

### Mapping procedure

1. Pull the top 5-8 ranked pain points from `PAIN-POINTS.md` (by frequency × emotional intensity × cost-of-inaction).
2. Pull the messaging pillars and competitive-differentiation angles from `POSITIONING.md`.
3. Cluster pains that share a root cause or a common "villain" (the thing standing between the ICP and the outcome they want).
4. Name each cluster as a pillar using the ICP's own language for the problem, not internal jargon.
5. Attach the positioning angle that resolves that cluster — this is what keeps the pillar from becoming generic industry commentary.

### Pillar Mapping Table (example — KOST DGR IATA training)

| Pillar | Pain Point(s) It Answers | Positioning Angle | Content Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Your DGR cert almost got you fined" | Fear of IATA/DGR non-compliance penalties; audit anxiety; expired certifications going unnoticed | First CBTA-certified center in Algeria — actual compliance authority, not a generic trainer | Build fear-relief credibility: show the real cost of non-compliance and how proper certification prevents it |
| "Why in-house DGR training fails audits" | Companies training internally with outdated material; HR/ops teams unsure if their trainer is even accredited | IATA CBTA accreditation as the differentiator vs. informal/unaccredited trainers | Position KOST as the credentialing authority, not just a course vendor |
| "What a DGR audit actually checks" | Uncertainty about what regulators/airlines actually inspect; last-minute panic before audits | Practical, operator-level expertise (not theoretical) | Demonstrate deep operational knowledge — proof of expertise |
| "Getting your whole ops team certified without stopping operations" | Logistics/HR pain around scheduling, batch training, cost per employee | Flexible session formats (in-person Algiers, planned regional sessions) | Remove practical/logistical objections, invite inbound scheduling questions |

A pillar with no pain-point citation gets cut. A pillar with no positioning angle attached is not differentiated — it is category education anyone could publish, and it will not be remembered as *yours*.

## 2. Content-Format Menu by Funnel Stage

Map formats to where the reader is in the funnel, not to what's easy to produce. A pillar can — and usually should — appear at every funnel stage in a different format.

| Funnel Stage | Reader's mental state | Best formats | Fits which pillars |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top-of-funnel (Awareness)** | Doesn't know they have this specific problem yet, or knows the pain but not the cause | Short LinkedIn posts naming the pain in blunt language; myth-busting posts; "3 signs you have X problem" lists; short native video | Pillars built on fear/urgency or misconception (e.g. "Your DGR cert almost got you fined") |
| **Mid-funnel (Proof)** | Knows the problem, is evaluating whether anyone can actually solve it | Case studies with named outcomes; before/after data; process breakdowns ("here's exactly what we check"); client testimonial video; long-form LinkedIn articles | Pillars built on expertise/credibility (e.g. "What a DGR audit actually checks") |
| **Bottom-of-funnel (Decision)** | Actively comparing options, looking for reasons to choose or reasons to hesitate | Comparison content (accredited vs. non-accredited, us vs. DIY, us vs. named alternative category); objection-handling posts; FAQ-style carousels; "how we're different" explainer | Pillars built on differentiation (e.g. accreditation, format flexibility) |
| **Guest / earned reach** | Cold audience, borrowed trust from a host or community | Podcast guesting, panel appearances, community AMAs, co-authored posts with adjacent (non-competing) experts | Any pillar, reframed for a specific outside audience |

Rule of thumb: if a piece of content could have been written by a competitor with the serial numbers filed off, it belongs to no pillar and should not be published. Specificity to the pillar's pain and positioning angle is the whole point.

## 3. "Content That Converts" Checklist

Run every piece of content — before publishing — against this checklist. Content that fails 2 or more of these gets engagement, not pipeline.

- [ ] **Specific pain named** — not "businesses struggle with compliance" but "ops managers get blindsided when an IATA auditor asks for a CBTA certificate their in-house trainer never mentioned"
- [ ] **Specific outcome shown** — a concrete result, not a vague promise ("zero non-conformities on the last 3 client audits," not "we help you stay compliant")
- [ ] **Proof or evidence included** — a number, a quote, a screenshot, a document, a named (or anonymized-but-real) example — never an unsupported claim
- [ ] **Clear next step** — one obvious, low-friction action (comment, DM, book a call, download) — not zero CTA and not three competing CTAs
- [ ] **Written in the ICP's language, not internal jargon** — check against the exact phrases in `PAIN-POINTS.md`
- [ ] **Traceable to exactly one pillar** — if it could belong to any pillar or none, it is too generic

**Generic content vs. converting content — side by side:**

| Generic (engagement, no pipeline) | Converts (pipeline) |
|---|---|
| "Compliance is more important than ever in today's logistics landscape." | "We reviewed 40 DGR training certificates last year. 11 were issued by trainers with no IATA accreditation — meaning the certificate itself wouldn't survive an audit. Here's how to check yours in 30 seconds." |
| "5 tips for better safety training" | "The exact 4 questions an IATA auditor asked our client last month — and the answer that saved them a grounding." |
| Reposting an industry statistic with no commentary | Industry statistic + "here's what this means for a 200-person logistics team in Algiers, and the one line item it changes in your budget" |

## 4. Cadence Template (Lightweight Weekly Rhythm)

This is a rhythm, not a full editorial calendar system — one owned post per weekday is often too aggressive for a small team; three consistent, high-quality posts beat five hollow ones.

| Day | Pillar rotation | Format | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Pillar 1 (rotate weekly) | Short pain-naming post or myth-bust | Top-of-funnel |
| **Wednesday** | Pillar 2 or 3 (rotate) | Proof post — mini case study, data point, or process breakdown | Mid-funnel |
| **Friday** | Rotate across all pillars | Comparison / objection-handling / FAQ | Bottom-of-funnel |
| **Monthly (1x)** | Best-performing pillar of the month | Long-form article or full case study, repurposed into 3-4 shorter posts | Mid-to-bottom |
| **Quarterly (1x)** | Cross-pillar | Podcast guesting or community AMA push (earned reach) | Top-of-funnel, borrowed audience |

Repurpose aggressively: one long-form case study becomes a LinkedIn carousel, three short posts, one newsletter section, and one talking point for the next podcast pitch. Do not treat each format as needing fresh material — treat cadence as consistent *distribution* of a smaller set of well-built assets.

## 5. Distribution Guidance

Content that isn't distributed is a diary entry. Split distribution into three lanes:

### Owned
- **LinkedIn (company + personal profiles of founders/experts)** — personal profiles of recognizable humans at the company consistently outperform company pages for trust-building; prioritize 1-2 named voices over a faceless brand account
- **Newsletter / email list** — lowest-effort highest-trust channel for existing leads; repurpose weekly posts into a monthly digest
- **Website resource hub / blog** — durable home for long-form proof content that search and AI answer engines can find later (SEO/AEO compounding value)

### Earned
- **Guest posts on adjacent (non-competing) industry publications or blogs** — borrows an audience that already trusts the host
- **Podcast guesting** — especially industry-specific shows the ICP already listens to; high trust transfer, low cost
- **Panels, webinars, community AMAs** — live formats that let expertise show in real time, harder to fake than a written post

### Communities (ties back to `caq-icp-architect`)
- If `ICP-PROFILE.md` has psychographic research on where the ICP actually spends time (LinkedIn groups, WhatsApp/Telegram industry groups, trade association forums, Slack/Discord communities, regional Facebook groups), **engage there directly** — comment, answer questions, share proof — before or alongside publishing owned content
- This is often higher-leverage than publishing on your own channel: authority built by being visibly helpful inside a community the ICP already trusts outranks authority claimed on a company's own page
- If `ICP-PROFILE.md` does not yet have this research, flag it as a gap and recommend running `caq-icp-architect` (or its psychographic section) before finalizing the distribution plan

## Error Handling

- **No `PAIN-POINTS.md` and no clear pain points describable from context:** Do not invent pillars from generic industry knowledge. Stop and recommend: "Run `caq-pain-point-builder` first — content pillars must be derived from evidenced pain points, not assumed ones. Generic thought-leadership content built on guessed pain points gets engagement but no pipeline."
- **`PAIN-POINTS.md` exists but has fewer than 3 ranked pains:** Proceed with what exists, but flag that fewer than 3-5 pillars may be possible and recommend expanding pain research before committing to a full quarter of content.
- **No `POSITIONING.md`:** Pillars can still be named from pain points alone, but flag that without a positioning angle, content risks being generic category education a competitor could publish identically. Recommend `caq-offer-positioning` before finalizing.
- **No `ICP-PROFILE.md`:** Distribution plan defaults to owned channels only (LinkedIn, newsletter, blog) with earned/community sections marked as gaps. Recommend `caq-icp-architect` to complete the psychographic/community-location research.
- **User has no existing content history to build from:** This is normal, not a blocker — proceed with the full plan; note in Next Steps that pillar performance should be reviewed after 4-6 weeks and reweighted based on what actually drives replies/inbound, not just likes.

### How this differs from `content-strategy` and `article-content`

Both of those are general-purpose content-planning skills — useful for any content goal (SEO traffic, brand awareness, editorial calendars, content operations at scale). **`caq-authority-content` is narrower and stricter by design**: every pillar must be derived from an evidenced pain point and positioning pillar already produced upstream in this suite, every piece of content is checked against the "content that converts" checklist before it counts as done, and the entire plan exists for one purpose — building enough trust with a specific, already-defined ICP that they raise their hand. If the goal is general content marketing, traffic, or editorial process, use `content-strategy` or `article-content` instead. If the goal is inbound demand from a specific ICP whose pains and positioning are already mapped, use this skill.
